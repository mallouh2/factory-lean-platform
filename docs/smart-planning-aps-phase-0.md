# Smart Planning APS Phase 0 business specification

**22 November 2026 · D1–D3 and G7a approved · APS implementation on hold**

This is the Factory Lean business reference for Advanced Planning and Scheduling (APS). It records the requirements approved in the Phase 0 brief. Technical proposals and unresolved choices remain in the companion [architecture and compatibility audit](smart-planning-aps-architecture.md) and [roadmap and acceptance criteria](smart-planning-aps-roadmap.md). The current product remains governed by its existing operational contracts and project rules. APS implementation remains on hold until Groups 6 and 7 are both completed and accepted. Each APS phase then needs its own explicit owner authorization and acceptance.

## Purpose and scope

Optimize delivery reliability, time, setups, startup and restart, waste, materials, finite capacity, storage, inventory investment and operational stability across the factory. Avoid unnecessary production and idle-time targets. Manual Planning stays available. Smart Planning is optional and progressively configurable.

Use plastic pipe extrusion as the first product and validation case. The architecture should accommodate continuous, batch and discrete production across plastics, aluminum window and door fabrication, food and beverage, pharmaceuticals, cardboard and packaging, and detergents and paint. Aluminum smelting and primary production, automotive, aerospace, petrochemicals, heavy industry, furniture, garments and other specialist industries are outside scope. Use shared capabilities and configurable characteristics across industries. Food and pharmaceutical shelf-life, GMP, allergens and detailed regulatory workflows require separate requirements and approval. Expiration-aware planning is a future concept. APS coverage conveys no regulatory compliance.

## Demand and combined production

- Net confirmed demand against usable, physically available, unallocated finished stock before creating a production requirement. Account separately for eligible incoming supply and its timing. Prevent duplicate demand, reservations and consumption.
- A future combined order groups child demand for a genuinely identical and interchangeable product. Preserve each child’s identity, quantity, priority, deadline and lineage. Child purposes include customer orders, required stock replenishment and optional stock. Different cut lengths or specifications retain distinct product and stock identities even when a line can switch between them without stopping. The initial APS trial requires identical product specifications and the same verified recipe revision. Quality-approved recipe equivalence is deferred.
- A technician sees one operational job per assigned run and records its physical production once. An authorized engineer, planner, Warehouse employee or other permitted user can inspect child allocation and progress. A shared order may split across eligible lines when the benefit justifies additional time and waste. Each line still has its own physical execution evidence. In APS 2, economic minimum run quantities are optional and configurable per product and line. Authorized Planning staff may override an economic minimum. Physical equipment limitations and valid production increments remain mandatory.
- Attribute accepted production to customer demand first, by approved priority, then earliest deadline, then oldest order among equal priorities and deadlines. Required replenishment follows customer demand. Optional or free stock is last. Within the same customer priority, rank undated demand after dated orders, show a warning and flag it for Sales follow-up. Reserve eligible accepted stock incrementally upon Warehouse receipt, respecting complete-shipment requirements. Authorized Planning staff may reallocate undelivered quantities when customer commitments are unaffected. Any reallocation affecting a commitment requires Sales approval. Record adjustments in the audit trail and protect quantities already delivered.
- A change of customer allocation during a continuous run creates no artificial setup, startup or downtime. Parent and child projections share the same production, Quality, material, scrap, reservation and Warehouse evidence. Existing same-shift reporting, corrections, permissions, stock, transfer and handover rules continue to apply.

## Setup, changeover, startup and restart

- Reuse available product attributes, recipes, units, tools and routing evidence. Let a factory choose additional relevant characteristics such as color, material, recipe, diameter, thickness, mold, tooling, packaging format and cut length. Support a no-change result, adjustment while running, physical setup, and mandatory cleaning or verification.
- Rules can be line- or equipment-specific, direction-dependent and conditional on combined characteristics. Apply the most specific approved applicable setup rule, count each activity once and flag unresolved rule conflicts. Separate expected time and scrap. Assume sequential setup activities unless parallel execution is explicitly configured and approved, with resource availability and mandatory prerequisites respected. A full product-pair matrix is unnecessary.
- Authorized Production engineers may directly approve and update setup and restart standards without mandatory second-person approval. Preserve permissions, effective dates, version history and audit records.
- Explicit zero and unknown are different. A qualified cut-length change may be configured as zero downtime and zero scrap only for the relevant line and conditions. White to black may require less cleaning than the reverse. Missing optional evidence leaves basic Planning available and limits savings claims. Missing evidence for active mandatory verification prevents a verified feasibility claim.
- Startup and restart are distinct from changeover. Forecast them from planned closures, holidays, prolonged inactivity, confirmed Maintenance, breakdowns and resumption after shutdown. Use configurable line-specific rules for startup and restart, including cold startup versus shorter recovery, stabilization time and expected scrap. Allow initial engineering estimates without historical data and improve them as actual operational evidence becomes available. Retained extrusion material may require a purge even with unchanged product and tooling.
- A shift boundary alone creates no restart. Avoid counting overlapping maintenance, setup and stabilization time or scrap twice. Reuse startup scrap evidence and have the forecast infer already available context. Actual duration, downtime, stabilization and waste require recorded evidence.

## Suggested schedules, customer deadlines and protection

- Consider confirmed demand and priority, eligible lines and rates, physical line configuration, reserved work, materials, Maintenance, calendars and holidays, transitions and restart. Consider warehouse capacity and financial policy when enabled. Group identical work and sequence related configurations if it benefits the factory. Compare lines on total time, setup, scrap, workload and suitability. Support optional line preferences and justified splits.
- Show one balanced recommendation. Reveal alternatives on demand. Exact optimization method, weights, thresholds and tie rules await owner review. Energy-cost optimization is deferred.
- Preserve requested delivery, estimated readiness and historical promised delivery separately. Prioritize customer commitments over minor gains. Allow a factory-default delivery buffer with order override and authorized urgency. In the first scheduling trial, an authorized planner may approve buffer use case by case for justified time or scrap savings while protecting customer commitments. Disclose the remaining buffer and added risk per customer before approval. Partial delivery requires customer- or order-level agreement. Early readiness alone grants no early shipment.
- In the approved example, day-2/day-4 conventional completions move to day 3 for both orders, against day-4/day-6 deadlines. Both remain on time and customer A retains one day. The feasibility and benefit still need evidence.
- Apply configurable protection to running, approved or pinned near-term work and honor confirmed reservations. The approved initial operational protection horizon is 12 hours, configurable by factory. Exceptions may be justified by identical demand joining active production, breakdown, shortage or a material delivery risk. Active extension requires safe capacity and materials, valid constraints and protection of other customers. Emergency override requires authorized approval and an audit reason. Proposals are advisory until explicitly approved.
- An accounting policy change has a dated effect and configurable transition period. The approved initial financial transition period is seven days and is configurable. Forecast the new policy when it becomes effective. Keep ordinary operational Planning available during the transition. Preserve the original approved plan, all approved revisions and actual execution separately.

## Supply, required replenishment and opportunistic production

- Keep Production Stock custody, shift reservations, material issue/return and actual consumption in their protected workflows. Consider confirmed purchase receipts by expected arrival. Distinguish future supply from physical stock. Raise shortage risks and propose purchase requests for approval. Purchases require the existing authorization.
- Warehouse capacity is optional and reflects occupancy over time. Include expected incoming production and actual or planned departures with stated timing. Readiness does not imply shipment. Product stock maximums and warehouse physical capacity are different limits.
- Show confirmed customer production, required replenishment and optional opportunities separately. A product maximum is a ceiling. Use forecast demand, shortages, available capacity, materials, storage, carrying period and finance to justify extra work. Stock production can be justified above the minimum and below the maximum or when no customer order exists. Unused capacity alone is insufficient.
- Optional stock belongs to a beneficial physical run window. It expires at an actual stop, shutdown or breakdown. Preserve produced stock and cancel, reduce or defer only unproduced quantities. Evaluate required replenishment independently. Reassess any post-stop opportunity as new.
- If a line completes A halfway through its last shift of the week, it may continue A against justified stock demand and start the three-day B run after the weekend if commitments allow. The A opportunity expires at shutdown. Urgent customer work may displace optional stock at the earliest safe point through authorized procedures.

## Financial ceiling, forecasts and setup

- Accounting owns an optional factory-wide working-capital ceiling in the factory's configured base accounting currency. Use its approved costing method where one exists and weighted-average inventory cost as the initial default otherwise. Value raw materials at inventory cost, WIP at accumulated cost and finished goods at manufacturing cost. Recognize internal conversion or transfers once. Receivables, sales value, cumulative spending and cash outflow are separate. Reuse existing costing where possible. A full accounting or cash-flow module is outside scope.
- Include owned in-transit inventory in current valuation. Project other confirmed purchases from their expected ownership-transfer date and carry that same value through receipt without a second increase. Missing cost is unknown. Accounting may approve estimates that are visibly marked. If material costs remain unknown, APS cannot certify compliance with a mandatory ceiling. Obtain owner approval on unresolved valuation and foreign-currency conversion rules before implementation.
- Evaluate inventory value through time using dated policy revisions. An illustrated reduction from 100,000 JOD to 70,000 JOD becomes effective next week. If opening stock exceeds it, disclose the breach and propose a feasible recovery. The new limit alone does not stop the factory.
- Accounting also specifies a maximum target holding period for optional stock, such as a week or month. Recommend stock only with reasonable evidence of sale or delivery within that period.
- Begin with simple explainable forecasts from historical demand, supported seasonality, confirmed orders and time-bounded Sales adjustments. Approved exceptional orders can be excluded analytically while their history remains intact. Prevent overlap between confirmed demand and forecasts. Distinguish observed sales from demand constrained by stockouts.
- For a new product, use an owner-selected comparable product with an adjustable reference estimate. Replace it progressively when sufficient actual history exists. Disclose inadequate evidence and forecast coverage. Recommend shortage prevention with enough lead time where supported. Do not fabricate stock demand or implement advanced AI forecasting.
- Design one shared Setup Wizard. Each department provides its inputs according to permissions and continues to edit them at their existing source. Optional CSV/Excel history is analytical input only. Detect duplicates, overlapping periods and unit inconsistencies. Track manual, imported, comparable-product and observed estimates separately, with confidence and missing data. Basic Planning works without historical or optional data. An enabled mandatory check requires sufficient verification evidence.

## Breakdown response and Sales

- Reuse Maintenance, transfer, return and protected replanning. With a credible estimate, compare waiting, transferring to a compatible line, or splitting remaining work. Include restart, scrap, setup, other work and delivery impact. With unknown or unreliable repair time, APS future planning treats the line as unavailable until Maintenance confirms return. Manual Planning retains its existing warnings and protections.
- At confirmed recovery, recompute options. Avoid automatic return transfers or fictitious actual downtime. Prioritize customer commitments and reduce optional work. Minor estimate changes need no alert, meaningful savings or buffer changes warrant a nonurgent recommendation, and material feasibility or delivery risks warrant an important alert and automatic alternatives. Repeat alerts only when material risk or recommended action changes. Actual transfer and plan changes require existing authorized procedures.
- Use Sales for a future pre-confirmation readiness inquiry using usable stock, materials, reservations and capacity. A quote or inquiry reserves nothing and leaves the approved plan intact. Confirmation rechecks current feasibility.
- On customer changes, net added demand against stock first, then check active and planned work. Reduce unproduced quantities where possible and protect actual output, Quality, consumption and shipments. Authorized users may reallocate eligible finished goods. Custom output is not assumed freely saleable. Assess new deadlines against capacity, storage, financial exposure and setup savings. Changes that affect the approved schedule require Planning approval. Delivered-order returns stay in their separate future workflow.

## Lean and experience

- Keep analytics in Lean. Compare conventional reference versus optimized proposal, approved optimized plan versus actual, and comparable periods before versus after adoption. Control for demand, availability, mix and constraints. Separate forecasts, realized improvements, recovered productive capacity and cash savings. Value savings only with supporting cost data.
- Track changeover, startup and restart counts and duration, expected and actual scrap, production duration, customer performance and buffer, inventory occupancy/value, forecast accuracy, idle capacity and avoidable gaps. Insufficient demand differs from operational loss. Use recorded evidence for causes and preserve assumptions, estimate versions and actionable history. Version improvements to estimates subject to approval and keep improvement targets distinct from scheduling estimates. Use the existing F37 plan/revisions/actual reporting requirement.
- Use the approved integrated visual language. Place Smart Planning in Planning and analysis in Lean. Show one recommendation and purposeful semantic colors with text and icons. Preserve existing Gantt labels, tooltips, zoom, exact positioning and mobile support. Use drawers and on-demand details, with English and Arabic UI and RTL. Keep technician recording focused. The Phase 0 concepts are documented without changing interfaces.
